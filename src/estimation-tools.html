Excel: confidence intervals for a mean

CONFIDENCE.NORM and CONFIDENCE.T return the margin of error, not a lower limit, upper limit, probability, or full width. Their first argument is α, not the confidence level; .05 requests 95% confidence. The normal function's SD must be the known population σ; the t function uses sample s.

Excel exercise: build a conversion interval two ways

Put 48 ones and 152 zeros in A2:A201. Validate that every intended record is numeric 0 or 1: COUNT excludes text and blanks. Do not count missing responses as no conversion. In a fresh sheet, set B2==COUNT(A2:A201), B3==AVERAGE(A2:A201), B4=.05.

  1. Using the built-in function, calculate B5==CONFIDENCE.NORM(B4,SQRT(B3*(1-B3)),B2).
  2. Without it, calculate B6==NORM.S.INV(1-B4/2)*SQRT(B3*(1-B3)/B2).
  3. Both margins should be .059189614. Lower and upper limits are =B3-B5 and =B3+B5: [.180810386, .299189614].
  4. Check successes =B2*B3 and failures =B2*(1-B3): 48 and 152. Report the assumptions and Wald limitations, not just the limits.

Here the normal function is being used to evaluate the Wald plug-in approximation, not because its SD is truly known. Zero/all conversions require caution; the function may reject a zero SD rather than reveal the uncertainty. Use the Wilson comparison in the lab.

Excel: plan the sample size

Mean: =ROUNDUP((NORM.S.INV(0.975)*18/3)^2,0) → 139. Proportion with historical .24 and four-percentage-point margin: =ROUNDUP(NORM.S.INV(0.975)^2*0.24*(1-0.24)/0.04^2,0) → 438. Replace .24 with .5 in both factors for the conservative result 601. Do not round the critical value or intermediate n before rounding the final sample size upward. Formula separators depend on regional Excel settings.
