1. Decide what one row means before calculating.

In our order book, one row is one purchase and the variable is its dollar value. That makes addition and averaging meaningful. A payment-method code of 1, 2 or 3 is different: the numbers name categories, so an average code of 1.8 tells the manager nothing useful. First identify the unit observed, the variable measured and its units. Then ask whether values are names, ordered categories, counts or measurements.

A frequency counts records; a relative frequency divides that count by the relevant total. If 30 of 40 online orders used a card, the share is 75% of online orders, not 75% of all orders. This denominator habit will become central to probability.

01Know your dataThe variable determines the sensible summary.+

Descriptive statistics summarizes the observations you have. Inferential statistics uses a sample and assumptions to learn about a wider population. A chart alone does not make an inference reliable.

Technical depth · Measurement scales

On an interval scale, equal differences are meaningful but zero is arbitrary (Celsius temperature). On a ratio scale, zero is meaningful and ratios make sense (duration, mass, order value). Taking means of ordinal scores assumes more than order alone: justify the spacing assumption.

Notation: {{math:count}} is the number of observations; {{math:observations}} are the observations; {{math:summation}} means add them. Floor rounds toward {{math:negative-infinity}}; ceiling rounds toward {{math:positive-infinity}}.

2. Choose the center to match the decision.

Think of the mean as redistributing the total equally across all orders. The original $540 total divided by 12 is $45. Think of the median as walking halfway through the sorted customers: positions 6 and 7 are $30 and $32, giving $31. Think of the mode as the most repeated purchase value, useful for a common price point. None is automatically the best “average.”

For these observed orders, mean × count reconstructs revenue exactly. For a profile of the middle purchase, the median resists the one large order. Forecasting future revenue still needs assumptions about whether future customers resemble these customers.

Pause and explain: If a genuine $200 order looks unusual, should you delete it to improve the average?

Compare your reasoning

No. Investigate whether it is an error or a different type of customer. A real large order belongs in total revenue. You can report both mean and median and explain why they differ, rather than deleting valid evidence.

02Choose a centerMean, median and mode answer different questions.+

Mean

Add the values, divide by their count.

{{math:mean}}

Uses every value and changes when a distant value changes. It preserves the total: n × mean = total.

Median

Sort the values and find the middle.

Odd n: one middle value
Even n, numeric data: average two middle values

Less sensitive to a few extreme values. It needs meaningful order. With ordinal categories, identify the middle category or categories rather than averaging category labels.

Mode

Find the value or category occurring most often.

{{math:mode}}

There may be several. Here, if every value occurs once, we report “no repeated mode,” matching Excel's no-repeat behavior.

Worked example: 1, 2, 3, 4, 50

Mean = 60/5 = 12. Median = 3. There is no repeated mode. Changing 50 back to 5 changes the mean to 3, but the median remains 3.

Technical depth · Not every average is arithmetic

Weighted mean: {{math:weighted-mean}}. With nonnegative weights and a positive total weight, observations can represent different quantities. For values 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and weights 6, 3, 2, 3, 2: 40/16 = 2.5. For grouped frequencies, sum over distinct values, not every observation again.

Geometric mean: {{math:geometric-mean}}, for positive values. For compound returns, use growth factors 1 + r, then subtract 1. Two one-year returns of 10% and 90% give 1.1 × 1.9 − 1 = 109% total growth, versus 125% for two 50% returns. The annual compound rate for the first option is √2.09 − 1 ≈ 44.57%.

Harmonic mean: {{math:harmonic-mean}}, generally for positive rates with equal numerator amounts. Equal-distance trips at 30 and 60 km/h average 40 km/h. The harmonic mean of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 is 2.1898. The weighting structure must match the question.

Outlier handling: Trimming removes observations under a stated rule; winsorizing caps values at stated thresholds. For 1, 2, 3, 4, 50, removing 50 gives 2.5; replacing 50 with 4 gives 2.8. These are illustrations, not recommendations. Check data quality, justify the rule and report the effect.

The mean balances signed deviations: {{math:balance}}. It minimizes the sum of squared deviations; a median minimizes the sum of absolute deviations.

3. A center does not tell you how dependable a process is.

A supplier averaging five days could deliver close to five every time, or swing between one and nine. A manager who must avoid stockouts cares about that difference. Spread measures departures from a center: the range uses the two endpoints; the IQR uses the middle half; standard deviation uses every observation.

To understand standard deviation, first subtract the mean from each value. Signed differences cancel, so square them before averaging, then take a square root to return to days or dollars. For 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, the mean is 3, the squared differences total 10, population variance is 10/5 = 2, and SD is √2 ≈ 1.41. This is a summary of distances, not a claim that every observation lies within one SD.

Technical depth · Quartiles and boxplots

Sort the n observations. The exclusive convention interpolates at rank p(n + 1). The inclusive convention interpolates at rank 1 + p(n − 1). With data 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6: exclusive Q₁ = 1.75 and Q₃ = 5.25; inclusive Q₁ = 2.25 and Q₃ = 4.75. The results differ by convention, not a calculation error.

For rank k + α, use {{math:interpolation}}. Exclusive percentiles are not defined when the requested rank lies outside the observed positions. This lab accepts at least four observations so its two exclusive quartiles exist.

A five-number summary is minimum, Q₁, median, Q₃, maximum. Some boxplots draw whiskers to min/max; a common alternative ends whiskers at the most extreme observed values within 1.5 × IQR of the box and plots the remaining points separately. Always identify which version is used.

Population variance: {{math:population-variance}}. Sample variance: {{math:sample-variance}}. Using the sample mean consumes one degree of freedom; under independent, identically distributed sampling with finite variance, the n − 1 version is unbiased for population variance. The sample SD itself is not generally unbiased.

4. Inspect the shape that center and spread can hide.

Our $200 purchase creates a long right side of the order-value plot. That helps explain why the mean is above the median. A similar SD can arise from different patterns: a broad balanced cloud, two clusters, or mostly ordinary values with a few extremes. Look for those patterns before reducing the graph to another number.

Skewness summarizes asymmetry and kurtosis is especially sensitive to extreme deviations. They supplement the plot; they do not certify a distribution. A flagged order is an invitation to check the record, not a statistical instruction to erase it.

04Notice shape and extremesA summary can hide an important tail.+

Skewness describes asymmetry through a standardized third moment. Positive skewness often goes with a longer right tail; negative skewness often goes with a longer left tail. Inspect the plot as well as the number.

Kurtosis uses the fourth power of deviations and is especially sensitive to extreme values. Excess kurtosis subtracts 3, the normal distribution's kurtosis. It is not simply a measure of peak height.

Two tempting shortcuts to avoid

Zero skewness does not prove symmetry or equality of mean and median. Zero excess kurtosis does not prove normality. A small dataset cannot reliably establish a population's tail behavior.

Transform with a purpose

A log transformation can compress large positive values. Square-root, cube-root, power, Box–Cox and Yeo–Johnson transformations serve different purposes and domains. Box–Cox requires positive values and uses {{math:log-transform}}; Yeo–Johnson can handle zero and negative values. A transformation is not guaranteed to remove skewness. Recheck the plot and explain the changed units and interpretation.

In finance and insurance, extreme outcomes can dominate losses. Lower historical volatility or kurtosis alone does not establish that an investment is safe, nor do historical averages guarantee future returns.

5. Move from one variable to two, keeping each pair intact.

To ask whether advertising and sales move together, each row must contain both measurements for the same branch and period. If a branch is above average on both measures, the two deviations multiply to a positive number. Above average on one and below on the other gives a negative product. Covariance combines these products; correlation rescales them so dollars versus thousands of dollars do not change its value.

Use “Positive,” “Negative,” and “A hidden curve” below. Predict the direction before reading the coefficient. In the curve example, explain how a strong pattern can have little linear correlation: upward and downward portions can cancel. A scatter plot is therefore part of the analysis, not a decoration after it.

05Read a relationshipAssociation is a clue, not a causal conclusion.+

Paired observations record two values on the same unit. Covariance asks whether the deviations from their means tend to have the same sign. Correlation standardizes that association to a unit-free number between −1 and 1.

PEARSON CORRELATION

A zero correlation can hide a strong nonlinear relationship. Correlation is undefined if either variable is constant. Always inspect the scatter plot.

Correlation does not establish causation.

Hot weather can increase both ice-cream and summer-clothing sales. Making one product sell more need not cause sales of the other. Randomization and credible research designs, with stated assumptions, help distinguish causal effects from confounding or reverse causality.

A complete first example

Read from top to bottom. print(...) displays an answer; a line beginning with # is a comment. :.4f displays four decimal places, without changing the stored calculation.

How the quartile code works

st.quantiles(...) returns three cut points: Q₁, the median and Q₃. q1, _, q3 = ... saves the first and last; the name _ marks the middle value we do not need here. Subtract Q₁ from Q₃ for the IQR. In Excel, the exclusive IQR is =QUARTILE.EXC(A2:A6,3)-QUARTILE.EXC(A2:A6,1).

Match the question, not just the function name

Population or sample? Use VAR.P / st.pvariance and STDEV.P / st.pstdev to describe the whole target population (divide by n). Use VAR.S / st.variance and STDEV.S / st.stdev to estimate population variation from a sample (variance divides by n − 1). The latter needs at least two values. Decide what population the data represent before choosing.

Exclusive or inclusive? Both are valid quartile conventions. We name the method explicitly in Python to match Excel. For these five values, exclusive gives an IQR of 3; inclusive gives 2. An unexplained difference is not necessarily a calculation error.

Why the mode needs special care

For this lesson, values that all occur once have no repeated mode. Excel returns #N/A. Python’s st.multimode(x) by itself returns all five values, and st.mode(x) returns the first. The guard in the reference checks for any repeated values before returning modes. With tied repeated modes, multimode returns all of them; Excel MODE.SNGL returns the lowest tied mode. Paired data: reproduce the correlation

Use x = [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] and y = [4, 7, 6, 4, 9], the same pairs as the relationship lesson and Excel cells A2:A6 / B2:B6. Do not sort the columns separately. Python’s st.covariance uses the sample denominator; the code converts its denominator explicitly to n. This gives the population-version summary of the observed pairs, not the unknown population covariance.

Pairs must have equal lengths with at least two observations. Pearson correlation requires nonconstant values in both lists. A positive correlation still does not establish causation.

Cell references, weights and paired data

A1 moves when copied. $A$1 fixes both row and column; $A1 fixes only the column; A$1 fixes only the row. Named ranges can make formulas easier to read.

Weights in B2:B6: =SUMPRODUCT(A2:A6,B2:B6)/SUM(B2:B6). Ensure the weight total is not zero and that the weights have a meaningful interpretation.

With y = 4, 7, 6, 4, 9 in B2:B6: =COVARIANCE.P(A2:A6,B2:B6) gives 1.4; =COVARIANCE.S(A2:A6,B2:B6) gives 1.75; =CORREL(A2:A6,B2:B6) gives about 0.5217.

Population CV: =STDEV.P(A2:A6)/AVERAGE(A2:A6), formatted as a percentage, gives 47.14%. Do not multiply by 100 again if using percentage formatting.

=SQRT(25) gives 5; =EXP(3) gives about 20.0855. Excel ROUNDDOWN rounds toward zero, while ROUNDUP rounds away from zero. They are not generally floor and ceiling for negative numbers.

Technical depth · Build a result without a summary function

  1. In B2 enter =A2-AVERAGE($A$2:$A$6) and fill down.
  2. In C2 enter =B2^2 and fill down.
  3. Compute population variance with =SUM(C2:C6)/COUNT(A2:A6).
  4. Change the denominator to COUNT(A2:A6)-1 for sample variance, then take the square root for sample SD.

Technical depth · Python shape summaries

The standard-library module has no skewness or kurtosis function. This teaching function follows the formulas in the shape lesson: population moments, corrected sample skewness (Excel SKEW) and corrected sample excess kurtosis (Excel KURT). It expects finite numeric values and rejects fewer than four observations or constant data. Sample skewness alone needs three observations; the combined function requires four because it also calculates sample excess kurtosis.

“Excess” means subtracting 3 from ordinary kurtosis. Population excess here is −1.3, while corrected sample excess is −1.2. The correction changes the statistic; it is not a rounding discrepancy.

Technical depth · Reconcile other libraries

NumPy’s var and std default to ddof=0 (divide by n). pandas Series.var and Series.std default to ddof=1 (divide by n − 1). NumPy’s default quantile method, linear, matches Excel’s inclusive interpolation; its weibull method matches exclusive interpolation where Excel defines the quantile. Python’s statistics.quantiles defaults to exclusive, so state the method explicitly. On very small datasets Python may extrapolate where Excel’s exclusive quartiles return an error. Use at least four observations for the quartile comparisons in this lesson.

Also check missing values, nonnumeric cells, finite inputs, paired-row alignment and bias corrections before comparing outputs. These examples deliberately use complete numeric data. Excel’s handling of blank/text cells is not the same as putting None or text into a Python list.

Technical depth · Build and weight it yourself

The Python counterpart to the manual population variance exercise is sum((v - st.mean(x)) ** 2 for v in x) / len(x). Change the denominator to len(x) - 1 for sample variance. ** 2 means “square.”

For nonnegative weights w with the same length as x and a positive total, the weighted mean is sum(v * weight for v, weight in zip(x, w)) / sum(w). Check equal lengths first: zip otherwise stops at the shorter list. Positive values are required for the geometric- and harmonic-mean comparisons here.
