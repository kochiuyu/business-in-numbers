Every example below is synthetic. Choose the question, direction and significance level before inspecting results. Excel formulas were checked algebraically and against SciPy, but were not executed in an installed Excel application. Python scripts and their recorded outputs were executed. These examples use different numbers from some interactive labs so you can practice transferring the method.

Excel: one-sample mean tests without losing the direction

Use a fresh sheet for each recipe. Enter B2=sample mean, B3=null mean, B4=SD, B5=sample size and B6=.05. For the known-σ filling-line case use 504, 500, 12, 36; for the unknown-σ service-time case use 27, 30, 6, 16. The SD must be a known population σ for z, a sample s for t.

Compute the signed statistic before the tail probability
Cell / purposeKnown-σ zUnknown-σ t
B7 statistic=(B2-B3)/(B4/SQRT(B5))
B8 left-tail p=NORM.S.DIST(B7,TRUE)=T.DIST(B7,B5-1,TRUE)
B9 right-tail p=NORM.S.DIST(-B7,TRUE)=T.DIST(-B7,B5-1,TRUE)
B10 two-tail p=2*NORM.S.DIST(-ABS(B7),TRUE)=T.DIST.2T(ABS(B7),B5-1)
Prespecified decision=IF(B9<=B6,"Reject H0","Fail to reject H0")=IF(B8<=B6,"Reject H0","Fail to reject H0")
Checkz=2; right-tail p=.022750t=−2; df=15; left-tail p=.031973

Z.TEST(data,null,sigma) returns a right-tail z p-value. Its two-sided conversion is =2*MIN(q,1-q), where q is the returned probability. Omitting sigma substitutes a sample SD but still uses the normal reference, not a small-sample t test. Excel has no direct one-sample T.TEST call; compute the signed t statistic as above. A low p-value does not give the probability that H0 is true.

Excel: independent means, pooled versus Welch

In a new sheet enter B2=42, B3=8, B4=25 for group 1 mean, sample SD and n; C2=37, C3=5, C4=20 for group 2. B5=0 is the null difference, B6=.05. These are independent samples. Pooled t assumes equal population variances; Welch does not. Do not choose the test by pretesting the variances.

Two-sided comparisons with a specified null difference
CellPooled recipeWelch recipe
B7 variance / squared SE=((B4-1)*B3^2+(C4-1)*C3^2)/(B4+C4-2)=B3^2/B4+C3^2/C4
B8 SE=SQRT(B7*(1/B4+1/C4))=SQRT(B7)
B9 signed t=(B2-C2-B5)/B8
B10 df=B4+C4-2=B7^2/((B3^2/B4)^2/(B4-1)+(C3^2/C4)^2/(C4-1))
B11 two-tail p=T.DIST.2T(ABS(B9),B10)=BETA.DIST(B10/(B10+B9^2),B10/2,0.5,TRUE)
Checkt=2.437120, df=43, p=.019017t=2.561578, df=40.855492956, p=.014208

The Welch beta-distribution identity preserves fractional df. Excel's T.DIST/T.DIST.2T functions truncate noninteger df, which can differ from SciPy; do not silently round Welch df. With B11 as the two-tail p, the right-tail p is =IF(B9>=0,B11/2,1-B11/2), and the left-tail p is =IF(B9<=0,B11/2,1-B11/2).

For raw observations, =T.TEST(group1,group2,2,2) requests the two-sided equal-variance test, and =T.TEST(group1,group2,2,3) the two-sided unequal-variance test, both for a zero null difference. With tails=1, T.TEST returns the small one-tail probability based on the magnitude of t; it is not automatically your prespecified directional p-value. Recover the direction using the signed mean difference. Validate raw ranges: COUNT ignores blanks and text; missing values are not zeros.

Extension: known population variances. If the SDs 8 and 5 really are known population SDs, use =(42-37)/SQRT(8^2/25+5^2/20) and =2*NORM.S.DIST(-ABS(2.5615775978928),TRUE) for two-tail p≈.010420. The same numeric SDs do not justify z when they are estimated sample SDs.

For the one-proportion test, check n·p0 and n·(1−p0): 50 and 150. For two proportions, check expected successes and failures in each group using the pooled estimate: all four exceed 10 here. A rule of 10 is a screening guide, not a guarantee. Small counts need an appropriate exact procedure, such as a binomial test for one proportion. Do not use the pooled null SE for the ordinary confidence interval: its unpooled Wald SE is =SQRT(B2*(1-B2)/240+C2*(1-C2)/200). This CI is not the exact inversion of the pooled test.

Excel: paired observations retain the matching

Enter before times 28, 32, 30, 35, 29, 31, 34, 33 in A2:A9 and after times 25, 30, 31, 31, 27, 30, 29, 31 in B2:B9, one matched person per row. Put =A2-B2 in C2 and fill through C9. Positive D means a reduction in time. Do not independently sort either column or discard missing cells without removing their corresponding pair.

  1. Signed t in E2: =AVERAGE(C2:C9)/(STDEV.S(C2:C9)/SQRT(COUNT(C2:C9))) → 3.473302.
  2. For prespecified H1: mean D>0, =T.DIST(-E2,COUNT(C2:C9)-1,TRUE) → .005180.
  3. Two-sided: =T.TEST(A2:A9,B2:B9,2,1) → .010360. The final argument 1 means paired, not independent.
  4. Assess normality of the differences for the exact small-sample t reference. There are 8 independent pairs and 7 df, not 16 observations and 14 df.

Excel: variance comparisons are asymmetric

For independent normal populations use n1=18, n2=22, sample variances s1²=36 and s2²=16. B2==36/16, B3=17, B4=21. The variance ratio is 2.25; do not square variances again.

  • Left-tail p: =F.DIST(B2,B3,B4,TRUE); right-tail p: =F.DIST.RT(B2,B3,B4).
  • Two-tail p: =MIN(1,2*MIN(F.DIST(B2,B3,B4,TRUE),F.DIST.RT(B2,B3,B4))) → .079758.
  • At α=.05, reject if F≤=F.INV(.025,B3,B4) (.384618) or F≥=F.INV.RT(.025,B3,B4) (2.483283).
  • F.TEST(group1,group2) returns a two-sided variance-test p-value. It does not return the F statistic or automatically supply a directional p-value.
  • Swapping groups requires swapping numerator/denominator df as well. The two-sided p stays the same. Normality matters strongly; this is not a robust general test of spread.

Extension: one normal-population variance. For n=20, s²=25 and H0: σ²=16, Q==(20-1)*25/16 gives 29.6875 with 19 df. Two-tail p is =MIN(1,2*MIN(CHISQ.DIST(29.6875,19,TRUE),CHISQ.DIST.RT(29.6875,19))) → .111825. This uses the equal-tail convention, not a symmetric absolute-value rule.

Excel Data Analysis ToolPak: verify the output labels

Choose the matching tool: “t-Test: Paired Two Sample for Means,” “t-Test: Two-Sample Assuming Equal Variances,” “t-Test: Two-Sample Assuming Unequal Variances,” or “F-Test Two-Sample for Variances.” Enter the correct ranges, labels option, hypothesized difference and α, then send output to an unused worksheet. The ToolPak F output is a one-tail display; do not report it as a two-sided p-value. Check the statistic sign and intended tail for every t output, and compare Welch df conventions before expecting an exact match. Installing/enabling the ToolPak is a separate local Excel step; no ToolPak run is claimed here. Formula separators and function names can vary with Excel locale.
